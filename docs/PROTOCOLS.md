# Numerical protocols and implementation details

This package generates the numerical results for the paper
**A Universal Iterated-Logarithm Anytime Law for Data-Driven Control Design**,
by **G. Calafiore**. The specifications below describe the Section 6 experiments.

## 1. Certificate conventions

The default confidence failure probability is delta = 0.05. The anytime
certificate is `AnytimeCertificate` in `code/certificates.py`. The universal
complexity weights are used for the empirical hull and deterministic endpoint
comparisons. A verified complexity ceiling gives uniform weights on
k = 0, ..., d: d = 2 for the scalar-state QP and d = 4 for the coordinate box.
The mechanical candidate bound uses d = 18, conditional on its reconstruction
premise.

CG23 is the one-sided fixed-sample equation in Section 6.1. The finite-horizon
allocation uses delta/N at each prefix through the prespecified cap N. The
summable allocation uses alpha_n = 1/log(n+e-1) - 1/log(n+e). Bounds are evaluated
with stable scalar roots; high-precision checks use an independent finite-binomial
expression. The endpoint sweep through 10^12 evaluates deterministic formulas.
Its points represent sample sizes, without simulating streams of that length.

## 2. QP stopping and continued inspection

Each observation is a scalar offset a, uniform on [-0.7, 0.7], and an independent
angle phi, uniform on [0, 2*pi). The design minimizes half the squared Euclidean
distance to L_des = (1.1, 0.7), subject to all prefix constraints
|a_i + b_i' L| <= 0.9 and L in [-2,2]^2, with b_i = (cos(phi_i), sin(phi_i)).
The exact evaluation risk is the formula in Section 6.2. All prefixes use the
same objective and full accumulated sample.

The first-target cohort has 10,000 streams of length 800 and seed 26090781.
Targets 0.05 and 0.15 were prespecified. Table 3 reports the first target. The
continued-inspection cohort has 10,000 streams of length 8,000 and seed 26090782;
Table 4 reports inspection through 800 and 8,000 within that same cohort. The
FH comparator receives N = 8,000 throughout this second experiment. Each stream
is counted once per inspection horizon when its true risk exceeds the tested
bound. The comparison tolerance is 10^-12; the accompanying audit checks for
comparisons within this tolerance.

Intervals are exact two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson binomial intervals. A zero
crossing count among 10,000 streams gives [0, 0.0369]% after rounding. These
intervals describe the finite Monte Carlo experiment, separately from the
analytical coverage theorem.

### Implementation checks for Section 6.2.2

The accelerated and polygon implementations are compared on 24,000 prefixes.
The reference computation gives identical support counts and maximum gain discrepancy
8.4e-16 after rounding. Sampled constraints are checked after redesign; the
reported maximum residual is 7.8e-16 across 144,741 redesigns. Exact current-run
values are available in the regenerated JSON outputs and run logs. These tests
assess implementation accuracy.

For the archive example, the first affected stream in replication order has
zero-based index 7. Its qualification begins at n = 271. A support-size increase

## 3. Retrospective performance-selection sensitivity

This subsection records the evaluator definitions used in Section 6.2.4.
It is an exploratory comparison. It uses 150 streams of length 800, seed
26090602, target epsilon = 0.05, and confidence failure probability delta = 0.05.
All gains are generated by the same QP rule above. After the full 800 observations,
selection minimizes a nominal infinite-horizon cost over prefixes whose own
anytime bound is at most epsilon. The reference is the first qualified prefix.

For a nominal plant x_(t+1) = a_0 x_t + b_0' u_t, starting at x_0 = 1, use stage
cost x_t^2 + 0.1 ||u_t||^2. With u_t = L x_t, its cost is

    J_0(L) = (1 + 0.1 ||L||^2) / (1 - (a_0 + b_0' L)^2)

when |a_0 + b_0' L| < 1, and infinity otherwise.

The **aligned evaluator** has a_0 = 0.8 and
b_0 = -L_des / ||L_des||. Its offset is outside the training range [-0.7,0.7];
it is used only as a performance evaluator. Its first and selected costs are in
`data/selection_sensitivity.csv`. The relative reduction in mean cost is
approximately 0.000897%, with one improved stream.

The **alternative evaluator** has a_0 = 0.6 and
b_0 = (0.7, -1.1) / ||L_des||. It was introduced after observing the near-zero
aligned result. In `data/lmi_replicates.csv`, method `Anytime` gives first
qualification and method `Posthoc` gives retrospective selection. Its relative
reduction in mean cost is approximately 3.38%, with 70 improved streams.

For either evaluator the reported percentage is

    100 * (1 - mean(selected costs) / mean(first-qualified costs)).

This differs from averaging each stream's relative reduction. Both quantities
are retained in the diagnostic outputs where available. Improved streams are
counted with a cost difference greater than 10^-10. The alternative comparison
illustrates evaluator sensitivity and remains exploratory; the aligned result
is retained in full.

## 4. Disturbance geometry and tube MPC

The stream consists of 8,000 disturbances, seed 26090601, sampled uniformly on
the parallelogram G[-1,1]^2 with G as specified in Section 6.3. The anchor is zero.
The hull and coordinate box are separate fixed design rules, each assessed at
(delta, epsilon) = (0.05, 0.02). A data-dependent choice between these two rules
would require a prespecified allocation across the rules.

The exact missing mass is computed by area. The reported nominal MPC cost is
an optimization objective at the stated deployment state, with horizon 20 and
terminal equality z_20 = 0. The invariant-tube support sums retain 160 terms and
include an explicit Lyapunov-norm remainder. `experiments.py` solves the MPC
problems at the first anytime-qualified prefixes. `third_review_figures.py`
independently solves them at the first FH-qualified prefixes and writes
`checks/horizon_mpc_verification.json`.

The ten-step certificate is 1-(1-U)^10. The corresponding exact
 disturbance-escape probability is 1-(1-V)^10. Under the deterministic tube
conditions, either quantity gives its respective bound on constraint failure.
The reported MPC costs concern the nominal constrained optimization problem.

## 5. Mechanical instance-level audit

The six-state, three-actuator model is specified in Section 6.4. The design
stream contains 800 plants, seed 26090761. Independent validation uses seed
26090762 and 50,000 fresh plants. The Lyapunov matrix is fixed, and the 18 feedback
entries are optimized using the spectral-norm form of the 12-by-12 contraction
LMI. The 64 uncertainty vertices verify a common strictly feasible gain for the
cube by convexity.

After each optimizer update, the script checks all sampled inequalities and
reconstructs the gain using the numerically identified support observations.
The maximum reconstruction error rounds to 4.3e-8, and the maximum stationarity
residual rounds to 1.2e-7. The smallest singular value of the audited active-gradient
matrices is at least 9.1e-4 after rounding, and the audited multipliers are
positive. Each update has a row in `checks/mechanical_audit.csv`.

These are finite checks of solved instances. Applying the paper's distribution-
free theorem to this sampling rule requires almost-sure support reconstruction.
The reported candidate bound at n = 322 has that conditional interpretation.
The output field `certificate_status` makes the qualification explicit.

Validation failures count **contraction/LMI violations**. The observed 34/50,000
failures give an estimate of 0.00068 and a two-sided 95% binomial interval
[0.0004709643, 0.0009501034]. This interval estimates the probability of failing
the prescribed sufficient contraction condition. A failed contraction condition
can occur for a closed-loop matrix that remains stable.
